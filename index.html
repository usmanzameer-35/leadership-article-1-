The article in plain English

This page gives the group the essential argument before anyone opens the full research paper or presentation.

The central idea
Coaching helped the managers understand themselves, but they still found it difficult to listen, respond and help other people grow.

The article therefore separates personal insight from interpersonal leadership skill. Both matter, but they are not the same thing.

11

Managers took part

They worked in a South African financial organisation. Each person completed ten individual coaching sessions over ten weeks.

  • Qualitative case study
  • Field notes and reflective essays
  • Six themes developed from the data

Why the study happened

The managers handled targets and technical work well, but many struggled with listening, emotions and relationships.

What improved

Participants became more engaged, emotionally aware and willing to take responsibility for their leadership choices.

What remained difficult

They still lacked confidence and skill in developmental conversations, empathy and helping colleagues grow.

Article glossary

Atlas

Twenty important terms from the article, translated into clear language for revision, presentation and questions.

20 terms
01

Positive psychology

The study of human strengths, wellbeing and the conditions that help people function effectively. It provides the foundation for the coaching programme.

02

Coaching psychology

The use of psychological knowledge and methods to support learning, performance and personal development through coaching.

03

Leadership coaching

Individual coaching that helps a manager understand and improve how they lead through people, relationships and workplace challenges.

04

Work engagement

A positive connection with work marked by energy, commitment and deep involvement. The article explains it through vigour, dedication and absorption.

05

Vigour

Having energy, resilience and a willingness to invest effort while working, even when difficulties arise.

06

Dedication

Strong involvement in work accompanied by significance, enthusiasm, inspiration and pride.

07

Absorption

Being fully concentrated and deeply involved in work, so that attention remains focused on the activity.

08

Learned resourcefulness

The ability to regulate thoughts, emotions and behaviour so that pressure and difficult demands can be handled constructively.

09

Sense of coherence

A way of viewing life and work as understandable, manageable and meaningful rather than chaotic or impossible to handle.

10

Comprehensibility

The belief that events and demands can be understood and make reasonable sense.

11

Manageability

The belief that suitable personal or external resources are available to meet the demands one faces.

12

Meaningfulness

The belief that a challenge has purpose and importance, making it worthy of commitment and effort.

13

Self-actualisation values

Values centred on growth, authenticity, responsibility, realising potential and building constructive relationships.

14

Internal locus of control

The belief that personal choices and actions can influence outcomes, rather than seeing events as controlled only by outside forces.

15

Self-authorisation

Giving oneself permission to act from personal judgment and responsibility instead of relying only on formal position or outside approval.

16

Emotional self-awareness

The ability to recognise, name and understand one's emotions and how they influence behaviour and relationships.

17

Interpersonal effectiveness

The ability to listen, respond, build trust and maintain productive relationships. This remained the weakest area after coaching.

18

Participatory action research

A research approach in which the researcher works with participants while also trying to improve practice. Cilliers both coached and studied the managers.

19

Discourse analysis

A qualitative method that examines language and patterns of meaning. It was used to interpret participants' accounts and develop the six themes.

20

Trustworthiness

The credibility and care of qualitative research. The study supported it through prolonged engagement and review by an independent psychologist.

Group preparation

Likely questions

Short answers grounded in the article. Open each question to review the recommended response.

Did the coaching programme work?

Partly. It improved self-awareness, engagement and personal responsibility. It did not develop interpersonal leadership skills to the same degree.

Why did the organisation need this programme?

The managers performed well on technical and financial tasks, but many struggled with listening, emotions and relationship-based leadership.

Why was the study qualitative?

Cilliers wanted to understand how participants experienced coaching and made sense of their leadership roles. Numerical scores would not provide the same depth.

What is the study's biggest limitation?

The sample included only eleven volunteers from one organisation. Cilliers also served as both coach and researcher, which may have influenced the data and interpretation.

What was the most important finding?

The participants became more aware of themselves, but many still could not use that awareness to listen effectively or help other people develop.

What should organisations do differently?

Combine coaching with observation, feedback, role-play and continued practice in real workplace conversations. Insight needs to become visible behaviour.
